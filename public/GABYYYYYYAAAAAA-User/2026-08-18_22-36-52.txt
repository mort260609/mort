  Your admin is right about one thing, and I'll say it before I complicate it. Webflow's Editor is genuinely easier than WordPress for day-to-day content updates. You click text on the page and change it. No dashboard, no plugin screens, no theme settings to get lost in. Since you're the one maintaining this, that's a real advantage and I won't argue against it.
  
  The complication is Arabic.
  
  Webflow has improved on right-to-left. Their own documentation confirms it now applies RTL direction automatically for Arabic locales, so the basics work out of the box. But independent analysis as recently as last month still documents constraints on bidirectional text rendering for Arabic and Hebrew, and most people building Arabic Webflow sites end up adding a third-party tool called RTLflow on top to handle it properly.
  
  WordPress has been doing RTL for well over a decade. It's mature, well-trodden, and there's nothing to work around.
  
  THE NUMBERS
  
  Webflow build: $2,900
  WordPress build: $2,500
  
  The $400 difference is the RTL work. On WordPress it's largely handled by the platform. On Webflow it needs per-locale style overrides, more custom CSS, probably RTLflow, and more testing to confirm the Arabic layout holds across every page.

  RECURRING, which matters more over time than the build does:
  
  Webflow charges for the site plan plus Localization, which is an add-on billed per locale. With three languages you're paying for two additional locales indefinitely. I'd ask you or your admin to check their current pricing directly rather than take a figure from me, because localization pricing is genuinely the thing people get caught out by and I'd rather you saw it from source.

  MY HONEST VIEW
  
  If Arabic weren't in the mix I'd tell you to take Webflow and enjoy the easier editing. It's a good platform and your admin's instinct is sound.
  
  But Arabic is in the mix, and it's the one requirement where Webflow asks you to accept documented limitations and add a third-party dependency. That's the risk you'd be carrying, and it sits on the part of the site that's hardest to fix later.
