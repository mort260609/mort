  The hard part is the inbox.
  You listed eleven capabilities as though they were roughly the same size. They are not, and the
  difference matters more than anything else in this document. Nine of them - records, stages, roles, a
  calendar, a dashboard, a job card that holds several trucks - are a well-understood build. The work
  is real but the risk is low, because every line of it is under our control and nothing outside can
  change the rules halfway through.
  The tenth, a genuine two-way inbox spanning WhatsApp and Telegram with images forwarded
  across the two, is where the cost, the risk and most of the calendar actually live. It is the only part of
  this system governed by Meta's platform rules rather than by our code: business verification,
  approved message templates, per-message charges and a strict limit on which conversations an
  integration is allowed to see at all. Any quote that prices your eleven items evenly comes from
  someone who has not built the tenth. This one splits the system along that line, which is why the
  plans divide where they do. § 06 on page 14 sets out exactly what each platform permits.

  Four principles.
  Four principles hold this build together. The first two are about making the premium land where a
  customer actually judges it, which is not always where the brand expects. The second two are about
  protecting what you have already specified, so that the palette, the typography and the control you
  are paying for all survive contact with a page builder and a commerce plugin.
  01 The riskiest part is proven first and built last.
  In week two, before the bulk of the fee is drawn, we build a throwaway spike that sends a real
  image from a Telegram group to a WhatsApp number through the live APIs. If a platform rule blocks
  it, we find out while the calendar and the budget can still absorb it. The inbox itself is then built late,
  on top of a system that already works without it, so a surprise from Meta delays one subsystem
  instead of the whole project.
  02 Every step in your chain becomes a state, not a screen.
  Lead, Sales, Customer, Payment, Job, Truck, Schedule, Programmer, Completion, Follow-up are
  not eleven pages to build. They are states on four or five records, and the interface is just a view
  onto them. Built that way, a stage you want renamed next year is a settings change rather than a
  rebuild, and nothing can sit in a state that the system does not know how to move out of.
  03 Nobody outside your office changes how they work.
  Programmers keep Telegram, WhatsApp, TeamViewer and AnyDesk. Customers keep the thread
  they already message you on and are never asked to install, register or log in to anything. The
  system adapts to the people who will not read a training document, because a tool that needs
  everyone else to change their habits is a tool that quietly stops being used by March.
  04 You own the system from the first commit.
  The repository is created inside your GitHub organisation, not ours. Hosting, database, Meta and
  Whop accounts are all opened in your name and paid on your card. At any milestone you can take
  the codebase to another team and lose nothing but our involvement. Given we cannot show you a

  HOW IT WORKS
  WHATSAPP, THROUGH META'S CLOUD API
  What it allows A verified business number can send and receive text, images and files, with everything
  arriving at the system by webhook. This is the supported route and the only one that does
  not risk the number being banned.
  The 24-hour window Once a customer messages you, your team can reply freely for twenty-four hours. Outside
  that window, business-initiated messages must use templates submitted to Meta for
  approval in advance, which is how the appointment reminders and follow-ups in this scope
  are built.
  What it costs Meta charges per delivered template message, priced by category and by the recipient's
  country, and updates the rate card quarterly. Replies inside the service window are
  currently free, though Meta has announced that service messages become chargeable
  from October 2026. Budget for it rather than be surprised by it.
  Keeping your app Coexistence lets one number run the WhatsApp Business app and the Cloud API at the
  same time, synchronised both ways. Your sales team can keep replying from their phones
  and the system still records every message. Throughput on a coexistence number is
  capped, which is irrelevant at your volume.
  What it will not do It cannot read a conversation held on somebody's personal WhatsApp account. No
  integration can. This is the constraint that shapes the programmer workflow in this
  proposal.
  TELEGRAM, THROUGH THE BOT API
  What it allows A bot receives everything sent to it directly, and everything posted in a group it belongs to.
  It sends text, images and documents back. There is no per-message charge and no
  approval process.
  Why programmers go
  here
  One group per job with the bot inside it makes the image forwarding you asked for
  automatic rather than manual, and free rather than metered. It is the cheapest and most
  reliable part of this whole integration layer.
  Limits File size ceilings apply to what a bot can retrieve and send. Anything over the limit is stored
  and passed on as a link instead, handled by the system rather than by your staff.
  WHOP, AND REMOTE ACCESS
  Payments Links are created through Whop's API with the job reference attached, and Whop notifies

  AnyDesk
