  # ORIN — Design & Customer Journey Audit
  
  **Prepared for:** Rajneet — ORIN Fine (www.orinfine.com)
  **References reviewed:** Mejuri (design language) · IDYL (customer journey) · Pandora (customer journey)
  **Prepared by:** Digital Heroes — Shreyansh Singh
  **Date:** 21 August 2026
  
  ---
  
  ## 1. Executive summary
  
  ORIN today is a beautifully written **pre-launch landing page**, not yet a store. The brand story is genuinely strong — the Core + Expression idea is clear, the founders' voice is warm and credible, and the material claims (solid 14k gold, D-colour VS lab-grown diamonds) are exactly the right trust signals for this price tier.
  
  What is missing is everything *after* the story. There is no catalogue, no product page, no cart, no size or care information, and no way for a visitor to see what "endless expressions" actually looks like on themselves. Every purchase intent currently leaves the site and lands on Kickstarter.
  
  That is not a criticism — it is correct for a campaign phase. But it means the redesign is not a re-skin. It is building the commerce layer that does not exist yet, on top of a brand layer that is already good.
  
  **The single most important finding:** your three references are not three versions of the same thing. Mejuri is your *aesthetic* reference. IDYL is your *structural* reference — it is the closest thing to ORIN that exists in the market and it has already solved the modular-jewellery merchandising problem. Pandora is your *scale* reference for gifting and configuration. We should borrow a different thing from each, and I have separated them below.
  
  ---
  
  ## 2. ORIN as it stands today — audit findings
  
  ### What is working
  
  | # | Observation |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | The proposition is legible in one line: start with a Core, add Expressions. Most modular jewellery brands take a paragraph to say this. |
  | 2 | The three-step "Transform Your Style" sequence is the right teaching device and sits above the fold-plus-one. |
  | 3 | The founders' section (Rajneet & Japneet) reads as authentic, not manufactured — this is a real differentiator at this price point. |
  | 4 | Materials block is specific: solid 14k gold, D colour / VS clarity lab-grown diamonds, precision-cut gemstones. Specificity is what converts in fine jewellery. |
  | 5 | Video hero and lifestyle photography are already at a luxury standard. |
  
  ### What is holding it back
  
  | # | Issue | Why it costs you |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | **No live catalogue.** On-site search returns no products. | There is no browsing, no filtering, no discovery, no SEO surface. Google has nothing to index beyond the homepage. |
  | 2 | **Every buy path exits to Kickstarter.** | You lose the customer, the analytics, the email capture and the cart. Post-campaign you will have no store to send returning backers to. |
  | 3 | **Navigation is anchor links only** (How It Works / The Collection / Concept). | There is no information architecture to grow into. Adding a fifth stone or a third Expression today has nowhere to live. |
  | 4 | **The core promise is never demonstrated interactively.** Four Cores and two Expressions are shown as flat tiles. | "Endless expressions" is asserted, not proven. The visitor has to imagine the combinations. This is the biggest conversion gap on the site. |
  | 5 | **No product detail page.** No pricing, no variant selection, no stock state, no delivery estimate. | Nothing to add to cart, nothing to share, nothing to retarget. |
  | 6 | **No trust infrastructure.** No shipping, returns, warranty, certification, care, FAQ or contact pages. | At £/$ several-hundred per piece, absence of a returns policy is an active objection, not a neutral gap. |
  | 7 | **Single testimonial.** One five-star entry. | Social proof at this volume reads as thinner than no social proof. |
  | 8 | **Metadata is unset.** The page title and social description are both just "ORIN". | Every link you share on WhatsApp, Instagram or LinkedIn renders as a blank card. Free traffic is being left on the table. |
  | 9 | **No size, fit or wear guidance.** Nothing explains how an Expression physically attaches, or what fits which ear. | This is the number-one pre-purchase question for a convertible system, and it is unanswered. |
  | 10 | **No email flow beyond a single waitlist form.** | The Founding 50 list is being collected but not nurtured. |

  ## 3. What each reference actually does
  
  I looked at mechanics, not moodboards. Here is what is worth taking from each.
  
  ### 3.1 IDYL — the structural blueprint (closest to ORIN)
  
  IDYL sells almost exactly your model: a base stud plus interchangeable add-ons, marketed as "The Convertibles". Their solutions are directly transferable.
  
  | Mechanic | What they do | Relevance to ORIN |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Base + add-on on one page** | The product page configures the base stud size, the metal, and single-vs-pair, then shows a running summary of the build before add-to-bag. | This is exactly the Core + Expression purchase. One page, one decision flow, one cart line. |
  | **Guardrail messaging** | If a shopper selects no base stud, an inline notice explains the add-on cannot be worn on its own and offers live chat. | Prevents the single biggest failure mode of a modular system: someone buying an Expression with no Core. |
  | **Category naming that teaches** | Their menu is literally structured as "Stud & Add-ons", "Hoop & Add-ons", "Chain & Add-ons". | Navigation itself explains the system. ORIN should navigate as "Cores" and "Expressions", not as "Shop All". |
  | **Size guide as a modal** | Carat-to-millimetre table with visual references, opened from the product page without leaving it. | ORIN needs the equivalent for Core stone sizes and Expression fit. |
  | **Trust stack at the top** | Free worldwide shipping, 30-day returns, luxury packaging, and a 2-year manufacturing warranty stated on every product page. | Cheap to implement, disproportionate effect on conversion. |
  | **Verified review volume** | Trustpilot rating and review count surfaced sitewide, not just on the homepage. | Your one testimonial becomes a review programme. |
  | **Shop-the-look** | Creator and customer photos link directly to the exact pieces worn. | The most natural way to prove "endless expressions" using content you are already shooting. |
  | **Gifting built into the cart** | Gift wrapping, handwritten card, and a free packaging gift are offered inside the cart drawer. | Fine jewellery is heavily gifted. This is revenue per order, not decoration. |
  | **Loyalty and account layer** | A club programme with points, plus a wishlist. | Repeat purchase is the entire economics of a modular system — every returning customer buys another Expression. |
  | **Deep brand pages** | Separate About, Design, Craft, Materials and Manifesto pages. | Your founders' story currently lives in one homepage block. It deserves its own architecture. |
  
  ### 3.2 Pandora — the gifting and configuration reference
  
  Pandora's relevance is not its visual style, which is far more mass-market than ORIN should be. It is their handling of two things:
  
  - **Build-your-own as a first-class journey.** A shopper can start from the base or start from the charm and the system assembles the rest. There is a dedicated entry point for "build", separate from "browse".
  - **Occasion-led gifting.** Gift discovery is organised by recipient, occasion and price band, not by product category — which is how gift-buyers actually think.
  - **Scale of merchandising.** Filtering, sorting, comparison and stock states that hold up across hundreds of SKUs.
  
  **What to take:** the *idea* of a dedicated builder entry point and an occasion-led gift path. **What to leave:** the density, the promotional badging and the sale-led tone. Those would undermine ORIN's positioning.
  
  ### 3.3 Mejuri — the design language reference
  
  This is your aesthetic north star and it matches the word you used: restrained.
  
  | Element | Mejuri's approach | What we adopt |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Palette** | Near-neutral. Warm off-whites and soft greys let gold and gemstone carry all the colour. | ORIN has four coloured stones — petrol blue, moss green, morganite peach, opal pink. A neutral shell makes those stones the only colour on the page. |
  | **Typography** | Quiet, generously spaced, low contrast between heading and body weight. Nothing shouts. | Replaces the current all-caps-heavy treatment with a calmer editorial hierarchy. |
  | **Whitespace** | Extremely generous. Products are given room rather than packed into grids. | Fewer products per row, larger imagery, more breathing space. Scarcity reads as luxury. |
  | **Photography** | Close-crop macro alternating with natural-light lifestyle. Skin, texture, real ears. | You already shoot this way — the site needs to frame it better. |
  | **Editorial hub** | A content section with styling guides, stacking advice and "how to wear" pieces. | ORIN's equivalent: how to build an Expression wardrobe. This is your SEO engine and it costs only writing. |
  | **Human help** | One-to-one styling chat with a real stylist. | A considered, low-volume equivalent — a styling enquiry form or booked consultation — fits a founding-50 brand well. |
  | **Services as content** | Piercing studios treated as a destination, with booking, aftercare and education. | ORIN's analogue is care, warranty and the Expression system itself, presented as a service rather than a policy page. |
  
  ---
  
  ## 4. The design direction I recommend
  
  **Positioning line for the build:** *Mejuri's restraint, IDYL's mechanics, ORIN's story.*
  
  - **Palette:** warm off-white base, a single deep neutral for text, gold used only as a functional accent — never as decoration. The four stone colours become the accent system, drawn from the product photography itself so the site shifts subtly as the collection grows.
  - **Typography:** a refined serif for display and a quiet grotesque for interface and body. Large, low-contrast headings, generous line height, minimal capitalisation. The current all-caps treatment is doing the work that spacing should be doing.
  - **Grid:** wide margins, two- and three-up product rows rather than four, and full-bleed imagery reserved for moments that earn it.
  - **Motion:** slow, short, few. Fades and gentle reveals only. Any motion that draws attention to itself competes with the jewellery.
  - **Imagery rules:** every Core shown on skin at macro range, every Expression shown both detached and attached. This is non-negotiable — it is how the product concept is communicated.
  - **Tone:** keep the founders' voice. It is the one thing none of your references have.
  
  ---
  
  ## 5. The customer journey we will build
  
  Six stages. Each maps to a specific problem the current site has.
  
  **1 — Arrive.** Hero states the system in one line and one motion, with two clear paths: *Build your piece* and *Explore the collection*. Today there is one path and it leads off-site.
  
  **2 — Understand.** The three-step explainer becomes interactive rather than illustrative — a shopper switches Expressions on a single Core and watches the piece change. This is the moment the concept sells itself, and it is currently the biggest missing asset on the site.
  
  **3 — Choose.** Two clean collection pages — Cores and Expressions — filterable by stone, metal and silhouette, with the pairing relationship visible from the grid so nobody buys an Expression with nothing to attach it to.
  
  **4 — Configure and commit.** A single product page that lets a shopper pick the Core, add one or more Expressions, see the total build and the total price, and add the whole set as one cart line. Guardrails borrowed from IDYL prevent orphan purchases. Size, fit and attachment answered inline, not in a policy page.
  
  **5 — Trust.** Shipping, returns, warranty, certification, care and contact — surfaced on the product page itself, not buried in the footer. Reviews collected automatically post-delivery so the single testimonial becomes a body of proof.
  
  **6 — Return.** This is where a modular brand actually makes money. Post-purchase email flow that introduces the *next* Expression, a wishlist so shoppers can save combinations, an account area that remembers which Core they own, and gifting paths for the occasions when someone buys an Expression for someone who already owns a Core.
  
  ---
  
  ## 6. Recommended sequencing
  
  Because you are mid-Kickstarter, I would not build all of this at once.
  
  **Phase 1 — Campaign support (now).**
  Redesign the existing page into the new visual language, fix metadata and share cards, build the interactive Core + Expression demonstration, strengthen the waitlist capture and add the first email flow. Everything still routes to Kickstarter — but the brand now looks like the price point, and the concept is finally demonstrated rather than described.
  
  **Phase 2 — The store (post-campaign).**
  Full catalogue, Cores and Expressions collections, the configurator product page, cart, checkout, size and care guides, trust pages, reviews and gifting.
  
  **Phase 3 — Retention.**
  Wishlist, accounts, the Expression-wardrobe email programme, the editorial hub and the loyalty layer.
  
  This way the campaign gets the benefit immediately and the commerce build lands when there is stock to sell.

  Proposed solution.
  Four principles govern this build. They exist because a multi-brand wellness store fails in ways a single-product
  store does not: thin product content, a catalogue that cannot grow, and an app stack that quietly eats the speed
  you asked us to protect.
  1 - Build on the theme you own, and go deep
  Impulse is a strong commerce theme and you have already paid for it. The value now is configuration depth, not
  replacement: sections built out properly, design tokens set to your brand, and custom work added only where
  Impulse genuinely stops. That keeps the store editable by you and keeps the budget in the build rather than in
  another licence.
  2 - Structure for brands and needs, not just categories
  A pet owner shops by animal, by need, and by the brand they already trust. Collections are built on all three
  axes, with filtering that still holds when you add brand eleven. Getting that structure right now is what lets the
  catalogue grow in the admin instead of in a second rebuild.
  3 - Reviews are content, not a widget
  Your Amazon reviews are the strongest trust asset you already own, and they only work if they land on the
  correct SKU with the correct star count. The mapping is the work; installing the app is not. We match them
  product by product and style the display to the theme rather than dropping a default widget below the fold.
  4 - Native Shopify first, apps only where they earn it
  Every app is a monthly cost and a performance tax. Discounts, shipping rules, email capture and promotions are
  configured natively wherever Shopify can carry them. We install apps for the review sync and for anything
  Shopify genuinely cannot do, and each one is named and agreed with its running cost before it goes near your
  store.
