  Your admin is right about one thing, and I'll say it before I complicate it. Webflow's Editor is genuinely easier than WordPress for day-to-day content updates. You click text on the page and change it. No dashboard, no plugin screens, no theme settings to get lost in. Since you're the one maintaining this, that's a real advantage and I won't argue against it. The complication is Arabic. Webflow has improved on right-to-left. Their own documentation confirms it now applies RTL direction automatically for Arabic locales, so the basics work out of the box. But independent analysis as recently as last month still documents constraints on bidirectional text rendering for Arabic and Hebrew, and most people building Arabic Webflow sites end up adding a third-party tool called RTLflow on top to handle it properly. WordPress has been doing RTL for well over a decade. It's mature, well-trodden, and there's nothing to work around. THE NUMBERS Webflow build: $2,900 WordPress build: $2,500 The $400 difference is the RTL work. On WordPress it's largely handled by the platform. On Webflow it needs per-locale style overrides, more custom CSS, probably RTLflow, and more testing to confirm the Arabic layout holds across every page.
