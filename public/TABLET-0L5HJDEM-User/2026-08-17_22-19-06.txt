  Deep-dive #2, as voted: how I built a complete forex broker platform from scratch — servers, bridges, matching engine, terminals, CRM. The whole stack. 🏗️👇
  
  Most "trading platform" projects are integrations — a UI on top of someone else's engine. This was the other thing: building the engine itself, where every bug costs somebody real money.
  
  The architecture, layer by layer (video walkthrough below):
  
  The servers — C++. Main Trade Server, History Server, Access Server, Backup Server. Separated so that a reporting query can never slow an order, and a crash can never lose the book.
  
  The liquidity layer. Custom data-feed repository integrating LP providers, plus MT4/5, LMAX, and PrimeXM bridges. This is where trading platforms quietly die — reconnects, sequence gaps, price sanity checks. The unglamorous 80%.
  
  The matching engine + risk dealer. Easy to write. Brutally hard to make correct under concurrency. Price-time priority, partial fills, and a risk dealer deciding what to internalize versus hedge.
  
  The terminals. Web, Kotlin Android, Swift iOS, desktop — plus Admin and Manager terminals in Qt. Social and copy trading built on the MT5 Manager API.
  
  The broker CRM. KYC, clients, IBs, transactions, trade reports, payment settings, roles, locations, login history, email logs. Boring? Yes. Also where the broker actually lives every day.
  
  Three lessons from building it: correctness beats cleverness when money moves. The bridges, not the engine, are where the hard bugs hide. And the backup server you hope is wasted effort is the one that saves the business.
  
  I build systems like this end to end — and I'm looking for one serious partnership: a founder, CEO, CTO, or investor with a real market. Remote: US, Germany, Netherlands, Cyprus, Europe.
  
  Fintech people: what's the hardest component in a trading stack, in your experience? 👇
