  # Combat & Progression Systems — Detailed Design v0.1
  
  Deep spec for the five systems the earlier docs referenced but did not define:
  **Attributes, Sigils, Rating, Contest, and the Session (Expedition) mechanic.**
  
  Companion to `character-assets-activity-design.md`. Every number here is chosen to
  uphold two invariants from the economic model:
  
  - **Skill dominates spend ≥ 3:1.** Power that money can touch is bounded; power that
    play unlocks is not.
  - **Every progression axis is reachable by a free player.** Money buys time-to-ceiling,
    never a higher ceiling.
  
  The mechanism that makes both true: **total power at a given level is fixed for
  everyone.** Only its *distribution* (attributes), a ≤10% *tuning layer* (sigils), and
  the player's *decisions* (session play) vary. Convergent power + divergent skill = the
  game is decided by play.
  
  ---
  
  ## 1. Attributes
  
  Four attributes. At each level a character gains a fixed pool of points; the **total is
  identical for every character of the same level** — only allocation differs.
  
  | Attribute | Governs | Combat effect (per point) |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Might** | Offensive magnitude | +1.5% ability damage / effect power |
  | **Focus** | Resource & precision | +1% Momentum regen, +0.5% crit, +0.5% status potency |
  | **Vigor** | Survivability | +2% max Health, +0.5% status resist |
  | **Tempo** | Action economy | +1% initiative, contributes to extra-action threshold |
  
  ### Point economy
  
  - **Start:** 5 in each attribute (20 base).
  - **Per level:** +3 points to allocate (L50 → 20 + 49×3 = **167 total points**).
  - **Per-attribute cap:** 60, so no character can dump everything into one stat — every
    build has a shape, not a spike.
  - **Re-spec:** allowed, cooldown-gated (7 days) or paid instantly with Essence. A paid
    re-spec is a healthy cash/Essence sink and the pressure valve for archetype rebalances
    (see open question in the activity doc).
  
  ### Why fixed totals matter
  
  A whale at L50 and a free player at L50 have the **same 167 points**. The whale reached
  L50 faster (bought time). Neither has more power available. The only differences left are
  *how the points are allocated* (skill of build) and *how the character is played* (skill
  of execution). This is the structural guarantee that the game is not pay-to-win, encoded
  in the attribute economy rather than promised in marketing.
  
  ### Allocation as expression
  
  There is no single correct spread — the right allocation depends on archetype, chosen
  loadout (§5), and the weekly modifier (§6). Examples at L20 (80 points over base):
  
  - **Burst Emberkin:** Might 45 / Focus 30 / Vigor 15 / Tempo 30 — win fast or die.
  - **Attrition Warden:** Vigor 50 / Focus 30 / Might 20 / Tempo 20 — outlast everything.
  - **Tempo Corsair:** Tempo 50 / Focus 35 / Might 25 / Vigor 10 — act twice before they act once.
  
  Reading an opponent's allocation from their snapshot (§7) and countering it is a core
  skill expression and a major input to Quality.
  
  ---
  
  ## 2. Sigils
  
  Sigils are the **durable, tradeable, ≤10%-aggregate tuning layer**. They never add
  abilities and never change what a build *can do* — only the numbers, plus identity.
  
  ### Anatomy
  
  Each sigil carries:
  
  | Slot | Content | Budget |
  |---|---|---|
  | Primary affix | One attribute or derived-stat bonus | ≤ 3.0% |
  | Minor affix ×0–2 | Smaller conditional bonuses | ≤ 1.0% each |
  | Visual | Frame, aura, character skin element | cosmetic only |
  | Type | Ember / Tide / Verdant / Gloom aligned, or Neutral | affinity, §2.4 |
  
  ### The 10% aggregate cap (hard)
  
  Three slots, so a naive max is `3 × (3.0 + 1.0 + 1.0) = 15%`. That is capped by a
  **global diminishing-returns curve applied to the summed sigil bonus:**
  
  ```
  effective_bonus = 10% × (1 − e^(−1.6 × raw_sum))
  ```
  
  `raw_sum` in fractional terms (0.15 = 15%). At raw 15%, effective ≈ 9.1%. At raw 8%,
  effective ≈ 7.2%. The curve makes the last percent of sigil power expensive and
  asymptotic — a perfectly-rolled full set is only marginally ahead of a decent one, so
  the market for top sigils stays a *collector's* premium, not a *power* arms race.
  This is what keeps sigils "expression and status, not power," in code.
  
  ### Rarity — affects roll ranges, never ceilings
  
  | Rarity | Minor affixes | Affix rolls land in | Issuance share |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Common | 0 | lower 50% of range | 70% |
  | Rare | 1 | middle 40% | 25% |
  | Fabled | 2 | upper 30%, better visuals | 5% |
  
  A Fabled sigil rolls *closer to* the cap and looks better; it cannot *exceed* the cap.
  Chasing Fabled is chasing the top of a bounded band plus prestige — desirable, tradeable,
  never mandatory.
  
  ### Affinity — the soft build incentive
  
  A sigil aligned to your archetype's Essence grants its listed bonus at 100%. An off-type
  sigil grants 80%. Neutral sigils grant 90% and fit any build. This gives sigils a *reason
  to match archetype* (so each archetype has its own demand curve) without hard-locking
  (so the cross-type sigil market still clears).
  
  ### Issuance — deterministic, announced, capped
  
  - **Seasonal contest rewards:** fixed table, published before the season.
  - **Seasonal shop rotation:** fixed quantities, announced counts, first-come at a set
    Essence price (not a randomized pull — see loot-box exclusion in the activity doc §14).
  - **No forging from raw Essence at will** — that would make sigil supply track total
    Essence and break the ≤20%-of-volume cap. Supply is issuance-controlled only.
  - **Re-roll:** an owned sigil's affix *values* can be re-rolled within its rarity band for
    an Essence cost (a sink); rarity and type are fixed. This gives sigil owners an Essence
    sink and a reason to keep trading without minting new sigils.
  
  ---
  
  ## 3. Rating
  
  An Elo derivative. Rating exists to (a) match fair async opponents, (b) band players for
  contests and for Quality normalization, and (c) resist smurfing.
  
  ### Update rule
  
  ```
  R' = R + K × (result − expected)
  expected = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_opp − R) / 400))
  ```
  
  - `result` ∈ {1 win, 0.5 draw, 0 loss}, per **ranked contest encounter**.
  - **K-factor:** 40 while provisional (first 15 ranked encounters), 24 in bands below
    1400, 16 at 1400+. Higher bands move slower, so the top of the ladder is stable.
  - **Async expedition PvP does NOT move rating.** It only *uses* rating for matchmaking.
    If ordinary farming moved rating, rating would inflate with playtime and stop meaning
    skill. Rating moves only in the weekly ranked contest (§4), where stakes are real.
  
  ### Bands
  
  | Band | Rating | Contest ladder | Approx. population |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Bronze | < 900 | Bronze | 20% |
  | Silver | 900–1099 | Silver | 30% |
  | Gold | 1100–1299 | Gold | 25% |
  | Platinum | 1300–1499 | Platinum | 17% |
  | Apex | 1500+ | Apex | 8% |
  
  Bands are also the normalization groups for Quality `Q` (activity doc §4) and the entry
  gate for contest ladders. A band with < 100 active players merges upward until it has
  enough for real competition.
  
  ### Decay & the smurf floor
  
  - **Inactivity decay:** −15/week after 2 idle weeks, and while Dormant.
  - **Peak floor:** rating never decays below `0.9 × lifetime_peak`. A player who reached
    1500 cannot sit at 950 farming Bronze. This is the anti-smurf mechanism — deliberate
    tanking is bounded, so a strong account cannot masquerade as a weak one to harvest
    low-band prizes.
  - **New account, real skill:** provisional K=40 climbs a genuinely strong new player out
    of Bronze in ~2 contests, so smurf-by-fresh-account is also short-lived.
  
  ### Matchmaking for async snapshots
  
  When building an expedition's 4 PvP encounters (§7), the server draws opponent snapshots
  from a rolling pool, filtered to `R ± 100` (widening if thin), and biased so the four
  opponents span a small spread rather than clustering — you should face a slightly easier,
  two even, and a slightly harder build each run, which makes Q a stable signal.
  
  ---
  
  ## 4. The Contest
  
  The weekly ranked event. This is the **only** rating-moving play and the primary
  non-market path to wealth (activity doc §10).
  
  ### Structure
  
  - Runs **Thu 00:00 → Sun 23:59** each season (season = 1 week; Mon–Wed is the casual/
    grind window, weekend is competitive — a natural engagement rhythm).
  - **Entry:** paid in Essence, priced per band (higher bands pay more). Entry Essence is
    **burned** — a sink, and the reason contests don't inflate the currency.
  - **Format:** a **Swiss-style set of 7 ranked encounters** against rating-matched live
    snapshots. Each encounter is a full combat (§5). Win/draw/loss feeds the Elo update.
  - One entry per character per week; re-entry not allowed (keeps the ladder honest).
  
  ### Scoring & prizes
  
  Final contest standing = points from the 7 encounters, tie-broken by aggregate Quality.
  Prizes are per-band, published pre-season:
  
  | Standing in band | Reward |
  |---|---|
  | Top 1% | Core ×3, guaranteed Fabled sigil, seasonal title |
  | 1–5% | Core ×2, Rare sigil roll |
  | 5–15% | Core ×1, Essence rebate |
  | 15–40% | Essence rebate (partial entry back) |
  | Bottom 60% | Rating movement only |
  
  Note the shape mirrors the economic model's ladder philosophy but pays in **game assets
  (Cores, sigils), never cash** — the contest is a skill outlet that *feeds* the market, it
  is not itself a cash prize pool (that structure was analysed and rejected in the economic
  model). Winners convert their Cores and sigils to cash *through the market* if they choose,
  at their own timing and risk.
  
  ### Why Swiss, not single-elimination
  
  Swiss gives every entrant all 7 games (more play for the entry fee → better value and
  retention), produces a fine-grained ranking rather than one champion, and matches you to
  similarly-performing opponents round by round, so Quality stays a clean signal across the
  whole field instead of collapsing after one bad matchup.
  
  ---
  
  ## 5. The Session (Expedition) — combat mechanism
  
  One session = one **Expedition**: 6 encounters, **5–8 minutes**, turn-based tactical.
  Production cap 10/week (activity doc). This section defines the actual combat.
  
  ### 5.1 Battlefield
  
  - **Three range bands:** Close / Mid / Far. Both combatants occupy one band each; the
    *distance* between them (0/1/2 bands) gates which abilities are usable.
  - No grid, no facing — range is the entire spatial dimension. This is deliberately
    minimal: enough tactical depth for real decisions, simple enough to resolve an async
    opponent's build convincingly by AI (the fidelity risk flagged in the activity doc).
  
  ### 5.2 Resources
  
  | Resource | Source | Use |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Health** | Vigor | Lose all → lose the encounter |
  | **Momentum** | +Focus regen/turn, +1 on a basic action | Spent to cast abilities |
  | **Initiative** | Tempo | Determines turn order; a Tempo threshold grants a rare extra action |
  
  ### 5.3 Turn structure
  
  Each turn a character has **2 Action Points (AP)**:
  
  | Action | AP | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ability | 1–2 | Cost printed on the ability; may require a range band |
  | Reposition | 1 | Move one range band (Close↔Mid↔Far) |
  | Basic strike | 1 | Small damage at current range, +1 Momentum |
  | Brace | 1 | +status resist and Momentum regen this turn |
  
  Initiative order is recomputed each round from Tempo + a small deterministic tiebreak
  (no RNG in ordering — the game reads as fair). Crit and status *potency* have a bounded
  random component; outcomes never hinge on a single coinflip because encounters run long
  enough (see length below) for variance to wash out — skill, not luck, decides.
  
  ### 5.4 Abilities & loadout
  
  - Each archetype has a **kit of 8 abilities** plus one **signature**.
  - Before an expedition the player picks a **loadout of 4 + the signature**. This
    pre-combat choice is a major skill/expression axis and the main hook the **weekly
    modifier** (§6) pulls on.
  - Abilities have **cooldowns** (in turns) and **range requirements**, creating the core
    tension: sequencing limited AP and Momentum across cooldowns while managing range.
  
  Archetype kit identities (each ~8 abilities themed to its Essence):
  
  | Archetype | Kit feel | Signature |
  |---|---|---|
  | Warden (Verdant) | Sustain, shields, zone denial, punish over-commit | *Bulwark* — convert incoming damage to Momentum for 2 turns |
  | Corsair (Tide) | Reposition, tempo swings, burst from Far→Close | *Riptide* — extra action + free reposition |
  | Emberkin (Ember) | Escalating damage, self-cost, snowball if unchecked | *Immolate* — huge damage, costs Health |
  | Shade (Gloom) | Status, information denial, conditional payoffs | *Eclipse* — hide own cooldowns from opponent for 3 turns |
  
  ### 5.5 Encounter composition (6 per expedition)
  
  | # | Type | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1–4 | Async PvP vs. live snapshots (§7), rating-matched | Skill signal, opponent-pool bot resistance |
  | 5 | Environmental challenge (weekly modifier applies hardest here) | Loadout flexibility test |
  | 6 | Apex encounter, tier-scaled | Main Quality driver (15% weight), the "boss" |
  
  ### 5.6 From play to Quality `Q`
  
  Every combat emits measurable telemetry that composes into `Q ∈ [0.5, 1.5]` per the
  activity doc's weights. Concretely:
  
  | Q component | Weight | Measured as |
  |---|---|---|
  | Encounters won | 40% | wins / 6, scaled |
  | Resource efficiency | 25% | (Health + unused-cooldown value) remaining vs. tier par |
  | Tempo | 20% | turns taken vs. par (fewer = better, floored to avoid all-in incentives) |
  | Apex performance | 15% | damage/margin vs. the encounter-6 par |
  
  `Q` is then **normalized within the player's rating band** so it measures performance
  *relative to matched peers*, not absolute — the reason skill creep doesn't inflate the
  currency (activity doc §5). Production = `10 × (1 + 0.02·L) × Q` per session, reproducing
  the economic model exactly.
  
  ### 5.7 Worked micro-example
  
  Corsair, L20, Tempo build. Apex encounter (Far start, hits hard at Close):
  
  1. Turn 1: Brace + basic (bank Momentum, read opponent opening) → +2 Momentum.
  2. Turn 2: *Riptide* signature → extra action, reposition Far→Mid→Close in one turn.
  3. Turn 3: 2-AP burst ability at Close while their heavy hitter is on cooldown.
  4. Manage: retreat to Mid on the turn their nuke comes off cooldown (range-deny it).
  
  Winning in 5 turns instead of 9, at 70% Health, with the signature used on-curve →
  high Tempo + efficiency scores → `Q ≈ 1.4`. A player who mashed the highest-damage
  button ignores range and cooldown windows, wins in 11 turns at 20% Health → `Q ≈ 0.8`.
  Same character, same attributes, same sigils — **the ~0.6 Q gap is pure execution,** and
  it is worth roughly +60 Essence/week. That gap, repeated weekly, is the whole "skill
  decides earnings" thesis made concrete.

  ## 6. Weekly modifier (the anti-solve mechanism)
  
  One global modifier per season, drawn from a pool of ~12, announced at season start.
  It reshapes the optimal loadout and attribute emphasis every week:
  
  | Example modifier | Forces |
  |---|---|
  | "Overcharge: Momentum regen +50%, cooldowns −1" | High-ability-density loadouts, Focus less critical |
  | "Attrition: healing halved, max Health +20%" | Long-game builds, Vigor and efficiency reward |
  | "Riptide: repositioning free, ranged damage +25%" | Kiting, Tempo, range-control mastery |
  | "Fracture: status effects +100% duration" | Shade rises, status-cleanse loadouts matter |
  
  Because the opponent pool (§7) is live players *also* adapting to the modifier, and the
  modifier rotates, no static script stays optimal for more than a week — the activity
  doc's bot-resistance claim (§12) rests on this plus the async pool.
  
  ---
  
  ## 7. Async PvP — how a snapshot fights
  
  The highest technical-risk system (activity doc §15). Spec:
  
  - When you finish an expedition, the server stores a **snapshot**: your character's
    level, attributes, sigils, chosen loadout, and a compact **behavior profile** derived
    from your recent play (aggression, preferred range, signature timing, target
    priority).
  - Other players' expeditions draw your snapshot as an opponent. An **AI policy** drives
    your snapshot using its own kit under the behavior profile — so it fights *like you
    tend to*, not optimally, not randomly.
  - **You are never inconvenienced:** your snapshot fighting for someone else costs you
    nothing and happens whether you're online or not.
  - The opponent you *face* is likewise a snapshot — you make live decisions against an
    AI-driven real build. This is what makes the strategy space equal to the live
    population's and impossible to hardcode against.
  
  **Fidelity is the make-or-break.** If snapshots feel like dummies, both fun and
  bot-resistance collapse. Mitigations to prototype first:
  - Behavior profile must capture *tendencies* richly enough that snapshots feel authored.
  - A small library of hand-tuned policies keyed to archetype+build clusters, blended with
    the individual profile, as a floor on competence.
  - Telemetry: track player win-rate vs. snapshots; if humans beat snapshots > ~65%, the
    AI is too weak and Q inflates — auto-flag for policy tuning.
  
  ---
  
  ## 8. How the five systems reinforce each other
  
  - **Attributes** set convergent power (everyone equal at level) → skill decides.
  - **Sigils** add a bounded ≤10% tuning/status layer → a market top-end without pay-to-win.
  - **Rating** matches fair opponents and bands the population → Quality is a clean signal
    and smurfing is bounded.
  - **Contest** is the only rating-moving, Core/sigil-minting event → a skill path to
    wealth that *feeds* the market instead of paying cash.
  - **Session play** turns execution into `Q`, `Q` into Essence, and Essence into the §1
    weekly choice — power or money.
  
  Every one of these is reachable by a free player and none can be bought past its cap.
  That is the same sentence the economic and activity docs make, expressed one layer down.
  
  ---
  
  ## 9. Open questions for this layer
  
  - **Combat depth vs. session length.** 6 encounters in 5–8 minutes = ~1 min each. Is a
    1-minute turn-based fight deep enough to separate skill reliably? Prototype the
    time-to-Q-signal: how many turns before a good and a bad player's telemetry diverge?
  - **Snapshot AI cost.** Driving a believable snapshot per encounter is real compute at
    scale. Budget it; consider precomputing policy weights per build cluster rather than
    per player.
  - **Attribute cap (60) vs. archetype identity.** Does 60 allow degenerate one-stat
    builds at high level? Re-check once the L50 point total (167) is play-tested.
  - **Sigil affinity numbers (100/90/80%).** Tune so the cross-type sigil market still
    clears — if off-type is too weak, four isolated sigil markets fragment liquidity.
  - **Modifier pool size (12).** Too few → meta solves mid-season across weeks; too many →
    players can't prepare. 12 is a starting guess; expand post-launch.

  # Asset & Activity Design v0.1
  
  Standalone product. Companion to `character-economy-model.md` — that doc sets the
  economics, this one specifies what assets actually are and what players actually do.
  Numbers here are calibrated to reproduce the economic model's curves exactly.
  
  ---
  
  ## 1. The core tension
  
  > Every week you hold a surplus of one resource and a deficit of three others.
  > You choose: **convert the surplus into levels, or into money.**
  
  That single decision is the game. Growth and earning are the same resource pointed in
  different directions, so a player is never idly accumulating — they are always
  choosing. Everything below exists to make that choice recur, matter, and stay
  interesting.
  
  ---
  
  ## 2. The character
  
  | Property | Detail |
  |---|---|
  | Archetype | One of 4, chosen at creation, permanent |
  | Level | 1–50, gates ability slots and production rate |
  | Tier | 5 tiers gating level bands (1–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–40, 41–50) |
  | Attributes | 4 stats, points allocated per level |
  | Sigil slots | 3, unlocked at L5 / L18 / L35 |
  | Condition | `Active` or `Dormant` (§9) |
  | Rating | Elo-like, drives matchmaking and contest banding |
  
  **Archetypes and alignment:**
  
  | Archetype | Aligned Essence | Playstyle |
  |---|---|---|
  | Warden | Verdant | Attrition, sustain, zone control |
  | Corsair | Tide | Tempo, mobility, burst windows |
  | Emberkin | Ember | High risk, escalating damage, self-cost |
  | Shade | Gloom | Information denial, disruption, conditional payoffs |
  
  Archetype determines *which* Essence you produce in surplus. It is the origin of every
  trade in the economy (§7).
  
  ---
  
  ## 3. The activity — one session
  
  A session is a single **Expedition**: 6 encounters, turn-based tactical, **5–8 minutes**.
  Cap of **10 sessions per week**. Beyond 10, sessions still play but produce nothing.
  
  **Encounter composition per expedition:**
  - 4 × asynchronous PvP — you fight *snapshots* of other live players' characters
    (their build and loadout, resolved by AI against your decisions). Matched by rating.
  - 1 × environmental challenge — rotating weekly modifier
  - 1 × apex encounter — scaled to your tier, the main Quality driver
  
  **Why asynchronous rather than live PvP:** no queue times, no scheduling, works across
  timezones, and no opponent is inconvenienced when you play badly. It also gives the
  strongest available bot defense — the opponent pool *is* the live player population,
  so the strategy space shifts every week on its own and can never be hardcoded (§12).
  
  **What a turn actually asks of you:** resource commitment under uncertainty (you can
  see the opponent's build, not their conditional triggers), ability sequencing with
  cooldown tension, and target prioritisation. Decisions are legible and consequential —
  a good player and a bad player reliably diverge, which is what makes Quality a real
  signal rather than a participation stamp.
  
  **Weekly modifier** rotates from a pool of ~12 (e.g. "burst damage reduced 40%",
  "status effects last twice as long", "healing disabled below 50%"). This forces
  loadout re-optimisation every week — it keeps the meta from solving, and it makes a
  week-old bot script stale.
  
  ---
  
  ## 4. Quality score `Q`
  
  Each expedition returns `Q ∈ [0.5, 1.5]`, mean 1.0 across the population.
  
  | Component | Weight | Measures |
  |---|---|---|
  | Encounters won | 40% | Raw outcome |
  | Resource efficiency | 25% | Health/cooldowns remaining vs. par for tier |
  | Tempo | 20% | Turns taken vs. par |
  | Apex performance | 15% | Result against the tier-scaled apex encounter |
  
  `Q` is **normalized within your rating band**, so it stays a comparable signal as the
  population's skill drifts upward. A player who improves gains `Q` only by improving
  *relative to peers* — which is also what prevents global production inflation from
  skill creep alone.
  
  ---
  
  ## 5. Production
  
  ```
  Essence per session = 10 × (1 + 0.02 × L) × Q
  ```
  
  Weekly, at 10 sessions and Q=1.0, this reproduces `P = 100 × (1 + 0.02L)` from the
  economic model exactly.
  
  **Split by type — the mechanism that creates the market:**
  
  | Essence | Share of your production |
  |---|---|
  | Aligned (your archetype's) | **70%** |
  | Each of the other three | 10% each |
  
  At L=20, Q=1.0: 140/week total → **98 aligned, 14 of each other type.**
  
  Skill matters enormously because upkeep is fixed while production is not. At L=20
  (upkeep 70/wk): `Q=0.5` → 70 produced, exactly break-even. `Q=1.5` → 210 produced,
  +140 surplus. **A strong player earns; a weak player treads water.** Grinding cannot
  substitute — the 10-session cap is hard.
  
  ---
  
  ## 6. Assets
  
  ### Essence — consumable, 4 types, the volume asset
  
  Ember, Tide, Verdant, Gloom. Fungible within type, freely tradeable, burned on every
  use. Target price band **$0.08–$0.12** per unit. This is the wheat.
  
  ### Core — consumable, scarce, tier advancement only
  
  Required to cross a tier boundary. Earned via a deterministic **Core Progress bar**
  filled by high-quality sessions:
  
  - Each session contributes `max(0, Q − 1.2) × 10` progress. A full bar (1.0) yields one Core.
  - A median player (occasional Q≈1.3) earns roughly **1 Core per 8–12 weeks**.
  - A strong player earns several times that — Cores are the main way skill converts into
    tradeable wealth.
  
  **Deliberately deterministic, not a drop chance.** A progress bar is transparent,
  non-addictive, and stays clear of loot-box regulation (§14). Expected price **$5–15**.
  
  ### Sigil — durable, supply-capped, the collectible top end
  
  - 3 equipped slots. Provide stat modifiers plus a visual identity.
  - **Total performance effect capped at ~10%.** Sigils are expression and status, not
    power. This is what keeps the game skill-decided rather than wallet-decided.
  - Issued only through seasonal contests and seasonal shop rotations, in fixed
    quantities announced in advance. Never randomized, never sold as a mystery pull.
  - The only durable asset and the only speculative one. **Hold to ≤20% of market volume.**
  
  ---
  
  ## 7. Costs — where the assets go
  
  ### Level-up
  
  ```
  cost(L → L+1) = 40 × L^0.6   total Essence, split evenly across all four types
  ```
  
  | Level | Total Essence | Per type |
  |---|---|---|
  | 5 → 6 | 105 | 26 |
  | 10 → 11 | 159 | 40 |
  | 20 → 21 | 241 | 60 |
  | 35 → 36 | 341 | 85 |
  | 49 → 50 | 415 | 104 |
  
  **The even four-way split is the whole trick.** You produce 70% one type but spend 25%
  each — so every player is structurally long their aligned Essence and short the other
  three, at every level, forever. Trade demand does not depend on wealth disparity,
  whales, or new signups. It is a comparative-advantage exchange between equals, which is
  both the healthiest kind and the highest volume.
  
  **Worked example, L=20 player, one week:**
  - Produces 98 aligned / 14 each other. Upkeep takes 17.5 of each type.
  - Net: **+80.5 aligned**, −3.5 of each other type.
  - To level once, needs 60 of each of the three off-types = 180 units bought, ≈216
    aligned at a 1.2 cross-rate.
  - Surplus is ~80/week → **~2.7 weeks per level**, or sell the surplus for ≈**$8/week**.
  
  That is the §1 choice, in numbers, every single week.
  
  ### Tier advancement
  
  | Tier gate | Cores required |
  |---|---|
  | T1 → T2 (L10) | 1 |
  | T2 → T3 (L20) | 2 |
  | T3 → T4 (L30) | 4 |
  | T4 → T5 (L40) | 7 |
  
  Cores are the long-horizon goal and the reason high-skill play stays valuable at every
  level.
  
  ### Transmute — the pressure valve
  
  Convert **3 units of any Essence → 1 unit of any other**, instantly, no market needed.
  
  This small mechanic does four jobs at once:
  
  1. **Guarantees the market is optional.** A player who never trades can still reach L50,
     just slower — this is the Diablo 3 test, passed by construction.
  2. **Hard-caps every cross-rate at 3.0.** No Essence type can ever be squeezed beyond it.
  3. **Acts as a sink** — burns 2 of every 3 units transmuted.
  4. **Self-balances archetype population.** An over-played archetype floods its Essence,
     the price drops, and playing an under-represented archetype becomes measurably more
     profitable. The population rebalances itself without any patch.
  
  Expect real cross-rates to settle around **1.1–1.4** in a healthy population. If any
  pair approaches 3.0, the market is broken and the governor should intervene.
  
  ---
  
  ## 8. Upkeep
  
  ```
  U = 20 + 2.5 × L    Essence per week, split evenly across the four types
  ```
  
  Charged weekly at season rollover, independent of activity. Level 20 → 70/week
  (17.5 per type). Upkeep alone is comfortably covered by a moderately active player;
  it is the floor that makes idle characters cost something, while **upgrades** are what
  actually drive trade volume.
  
  ## 9. Dormancy — what happens when you don't pay
  
  Unpaid upkeep does **not** destroy the character. It goes **Dormant**:
  
  - Production stops, rating decays slowly, character cannot enter contests.
  - Nothing is lost. Levels, Sigils, and Cores are untouched.
  - Reactivate by paying arrears, **capped at 4 weeks** regardless of absence length.
  
  Never destroy something a user paid real money for. A returning player after six months
  owes four weeks, not twenty-six — a punitive reactivation cost simply converts a lapsed
  player into a permanently lost one, and destroying purchased value invites exactly the
  consumer-protection attention the model can't afford.
  
  ---
  
  ## 10. The weekly contest
  
  Runs each season, entry paid in **Essence** (never cash — see the economic model's
  prize-pool analysis for why the cash-entry version was abandoned).
  
  - Banded by rating; separate ladders per band.
  - Prizes: Cores, Sigil issuance, cosmetic titles.
  - Entry fees are **burned**, making the contest a meaningful sink as well as the
    primary skill outlet.
  
  This gives high-skill players a non-market path to wealth, which matters: without it,
  the only way to get rich is to trade well, and the game quietly becomes a trading
  game rather than a game with a market.
  
  ---
  
  ## 11. Market mechanics
  
  - **Order book per asset**: 4 Essence books, 1 Core book, 1 book per Sigil type.
  - **Limit orders only.** No "sell instantly to the system" — the server never takes
    the other side, per the economic model's rule that it taxes but never sells.
  - **8% fee on sale proceeds**, plus a small non-refundable listing fee to suppress
    order-book spam and layering.
  - **Settlement is atomic** with balance escrow at order placement.
  - Price history and depth are public. Opaque markets get assumed to be rigged.
  - **Anti-manipulation:** wash-trade detection on trade graphs, per-account position
    limits on Cores and Sigils, and cancel-rate throttling.
  
  ---
  
  ## 12. How this activity design defeats bots
  
  The economic defense is in the economic model (capital cost vs. yield, 6.3-week
  payback). The *activity* design contributes four things:
  
  1. **The 10-session weekly cap.** A bot cannot out-grind a human. Scaling requires more
     accounts, and each account costs real capital — botting becomes capital-bound, which
     is exactly where the payback math kills it.
  2. **Async PvP against live player builds.** The opponent distribution is the player
     population, so the optimal strategy drifts continuously with no patch required.
  3. **Weekly modifiers.** A solved script is stale within seven days.
  4. **Quality-weighted output.** A crude bot completes expeditions at Q≈0.6 — barely
     above upkeep. Botting doesn't just risk a ban, it earns badly, and low-yield
     attacks aren't worth automating.
  
  ## 13. New player, first four weeks
  
  | Week | Level | Experience |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 1 → 6 | Free character. Upkeep trivially covered. Learns the loop, no market contact. |
  | 2 | 6 → 9 | First off-type deficit appears. Transmute is offered — deliberately, so the friction is felt before the market is introduced. |
  | 3 | 9 → 11 | Market unlocks. First trade is a guided flow: sell surplus aligned Essence, buy what's short. First Core, first tier gate. |
  | 4 | 11 → 13 | First contest entry. Earning is now visible and real, at a realistic scale. |
  
  Withdrawal remains gated at $50 with KYC, so a new player meets the market as a
  *player* well before they meet it as an earner. That ordering is deliberate: it
  establishes the game as the product and the earning as a consequence, which is both
  better retention and a materially better legal posture than the reverse.
  
  ---
  
  ## 14. Explicitly excluded
  
  | Excluded | Why |
  |---|---|
  | Loot boxes / randomized paid rewards | Regulated as gambling in several jurisdictions (BE, NL, and growing). Core Progress and announced Sigil issuance are deterministic by design. |
  | Pay-to-win power | Sigils capped at ~10% effect; power comes from level and skill, both reachable without trading. |
  | Idle / passive production | Passive yield on a purchased asset is the fact pattern that makes an item a security. Production requires active play, always. |
  | Server-side asset sales into the market | The server would compete with its own users and cap the price ceiling it advertises. |
  | Character destruction or permanent loss | Never destroy purchased value. Dormancy instead. |
  | Displayed ROI, APY, or payback estimates | The fastest route to a securities characterization. |
  
  These are compliance and design invariants, not preferences. Each deserves a test that
  fails loudly if a future feature violates it.
  
  ---
  
  ## 15. Open questions
  
  - **Session length vs. cap.** 10 × 6min = ~1 hour/week to max production. Likely too
    low for engagement — consider decoupling *production* (capped) from *rating and
    contest progress* (uncapped), so dedicated players have somewhere to put extra hours
    that doesn't inflate the currency supply.
  - **Archetype permanence.** Permanent choice deepens identity and makes the trade
    structure legible, but strands players when a rebalance hits. A paid, cooldown-gated
    re-spec is probably necessary — and is itself a good cash sink.
  - **Async PvP fidelity.** Resolving a snapshot build by AI must feel like fighting a
    person. If it feels like fighting a dummy, the bot-resistance argument in §12 weakens
    along with the fun. Prototype this first — it is the highest technical risk in the design.
  - **Cross-rate stability at low population.** Four separate order books need roughly 4×
    the liquidity of one. Consider launching with 2 Essence types and splitting to 4 once
    trader count clears the viability floor.
