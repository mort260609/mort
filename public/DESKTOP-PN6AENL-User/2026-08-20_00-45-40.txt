  Recently, I was coaching a Treatment Coordinator who had no clinical dental background.
  
  She was expected to discuss treatment plans with patients, but like many people who come into a TC or front desk role without clinical experience, she was looking at things like:
  
  16
  532
  688
  671
  324
  
  And essentially trying to interpret another language.
  
  It made me think...
  
  How can we expect someone to confidently explain a treatment plan to a patient if they don't fully understand what they're reading themselves?
  
  So we went back to basics.
  
  What's the tooth number telling me?
  
  Where is that tooth?
  
  What's the item number telling me?
  
  What type of treatment are we talking about?
  
  And most importantly...
  
  How do I translate all of that into normal human language for the patient?
  
  That's what led me to create two simple resources:
  
  Tooth Numbers in Human Language
  
  and
  
  Dental Item Numbers at a Glance
  
  Not to turn a Treatment Coordinator into a clinician.
  
  Not to teach them to diagnose or treatment plan.
  
  But to give someone without a clinical background enough foundational knowledge to understand the information they're communicating instead of simply repeating dental terminology and numbers.
  
  Because you can't confidently transfer knowledge you don't have.
