  SECTION — WHY WE EXIST
  
  Operation Stable Ground was created around a simple belief:
  
  Veterans should not have to hit rock bottom before someone helps them transition.
  
  There are programs designed to respond after homelessness, unemployment, isolation, financial problems, or other crises have already taken hold.
  
  We want to intervene earlier.
  
  Our initial focus is on veterans recently separated from military service who are experiencing or at meaningful risk of housing instability, unemployment, underemployment, financial difficulty, social isolation, or other transition barriers.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — OUR VALUES
  
  Display five large values.
  
  DISCIPLINE
  
  Progress requires consistency and accountability.
  
  INTEGRITY
  
  We do what we say we are going to do.
  
  LEADERSHIP
  
  Leadership does not end when military service does.
  
  CONFIDENCE
  
  Veterans should leave believing they are capable of building their next chapter.
  
  RESILIENCE
  
  Setbacks are part of transition. Giving up does not have to be.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — WHAT OSG IS NOT
  
  Operation Stable Ground is not intended to replace:
  
  * The Department of Veterans Affairs
  * Licensed mental-health providers
  * Medical providers
  * Accredited VA claims representatives
  * Workforce agencies
  * Colleges or trade schools
  * Permanent housing providers
  
  Instead, OSG is designed to help veterans connect those resources together and actually follow through on them.

  PAGE 3 — THE PROGRAM
  
  HERO
  
  Heading
  
  90 DAYS TO BUILD THE NEXT MISSION.
  
  Supporting Copy
  
  The Operation Stable Ground 90-Day Veteran Transition Program combines private transitional housing with structure, individualized planning, career development, financial education, benefits navigation, mentorship, wellness, meals, transportation and housing-transition support.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — PROGRAM AT A GLANCE
  
  Length: Up to 90 calendar days
  
  Core Curriculum: 12 weeks / 84 days
  
  Final Transition Period: Up to 6 additional days
  
  Cohort Size: Up to 9 veterans
  
  Annual Initial Capacity: Approximately 34 veterans
  
  Housing: Private furnished cottages
  
  Location: Planned Illinois campus
  
  Participant Housing Fee at Launch: $0
  
  Case Management Goal: Approximately 1 primary case manager per 10 active residents or better
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — THE 90-DAY JOURNEY
  
  Use a horizontal timeline.
  
  PHASE 1 — STABILIZE
  
  Days 1–14
  
  The first priority is understanding where the veteran currently stands.
  
  Focus areas include:
  
  * Initial assessment
  * Individual Transition Plan
  * Identification and documentation
  * Benefits review
  * Financial assessment
  * Employment assessment
  * Housing assessment
  * Immediate needs
  * Personal goals
  * Establishing routine
  
  PHASE 2 — BUILD
  
  Days 15–45
  
  Participants begin turning goals into measurable action.
  
  Focus areas include:
  
  * Resume development
  * Civilian job translation
  * Applications
  * Interview preparation
  * Financial literacy
  * Credit and budgeting
  * Education/training exploration
  * Benefits appointments
  * Communication skills
  * Life skills
  * Mentorship
  
  PHASE 3 — EXECUTE
  
  Days 46–84
  
  The emphasis moves toward implementation.
  
  Participants work toward:
  
  * Employment offers
  * School or training enrollment
  * Housing applications
  * Benefits connections
  * Savings and budgeting
  * Transportation planning
  * Community connections
  * Permanent support systems
  
  PHASE 4 — TRANSITION
  
  Days 85–90
  
  The final days focus on execution rather than classroom instruction.
  
  This can include:
  
  * Move-out
  * Final appointments
  * Housing transition
  * Document transfer
  * Transportation confirmation
  * Employer or school handoffs
  * Community-resource handoffs
  * Final transition review

  Veterans may qualify based on issues including:
  
  • Homelessness or imminent homelessness
  • Temporary or unstable housing
  • Unemployment
  • Underemployment
  • Difficulty translating military experience
  • Financial instability
  • Limited support network
  • Social isolation
  • Difficulty accessing veteran resources
  • Need for civilian routine and structure
  • Other documented transition barriers
  
  ────────
  
  SECTION — HEALTH CONDITIONS
  
  Heading
  
  A DIAGNOSIS DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY DISQUALIFY A VETERAN.
  
  Veterans experiencing PTSD, depression, anxiety, physical disabilities, service-connected disabilities or other conditions will not be excluded solely because of a diagnosis.

  A nonviolent criminal record does not automatically disqualify an applicant.
  
  OSG intends to use individualized safety and suitability review considering the circumstances, recency, rehabilitation, legal restrictions and ability of the program to safely support the applicant.

  Mission Statement

  SECTION — WHY WE EXIST
  
  Operation Stable Ground was created around a simple belief:

  There are programs designed to respond after homelessness, unemployment, isolation, financial problems, or other crises have already taken hold.
  
  We want to intervene earlier.
  
  Our initial focus is on veterans recently separated from military service who are experiencing or at meaningful risk of housing instability, unemployment, underemployment, financial difficulty, social isolation, or other transition barriers.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — OUR VALUES
  
  Display five large values.
  
  DISCIPLINE
  
  Progress requires consistency and accountability.
  
  INTEGRITY
  
  We do what we say we are going to do.
  
  LEADERSHIP
  
  Leadership does not end when military service does.
  
  CONFIDENCE
  
  Veterans should leave believing they are capable of building their next chapter.
  
  RESILIENCE
  
  Setbacks are part of transition. Giving up does not have to be.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — WHAT OSG IS NOT
  
  Operation Stable Ground is not intended to replace:
  
  * The Department of Veterans Affairs
  * Licensed mental-health providers
  * Medical providers
  * Accredited VA claims representatives
  * Workforce agencies
  * Colleges or trade schools
  * Permanent housing providers
  
  Instead, OSG is designed to help veterans connect those resources together and actually follow through on them.
  
  
  
  PAGE 3 — THE PROGRAM
  
  HERO
  
  Heading
  
  90 DAYS TO BUILD THE NEXT MISSION.
  
  Supporting Copy
  
  The Operation Stable Ground 90-Day Veteran Transition Program combines private transitional housing with structure, individualized planning, career development, financial education, benefits navigation, mentorship, wellness, meals, transportation and housing-transition support.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — PROGRAM AT A GLANCE
  
  Length: Up to 90 calendar days
  
  Core Curriculum: 12 weeks / 84 days
  
  Final Transition Period: Up to 6 additional days
  
  Cohort Size: Up to 9 veterans
  
  Annual Initial Capacity: Approximately 34 veterans
  
  Housing: Private furnished cottages
  
  Location: Planned Illinois campus
  
  Participant Housing Fee at Launch: $0
  
  Case Management Goal: Approximately 1 primary case manager per 10 active residents or better
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — THE 90-DAY JOURNEY
  
  Use a horizontal timeline.
  
  PHASE 1 — STABILIZE
  
  Days 1–14
  
  The first priority is understanding where the veteran currently stands.
  
  Focus areas include:
  
  * Initial assessment
  * Individual Transition Plan
  * Identification and documentation
  * Benefits review
  * Financial assessment
  * Employment assessment
  * Housing assessment
  * Immediate needs
  * Personal goals
  * Establishing routine
  
  PHASE 2 — BUILD
  
  Days 15–45
  
  Participants begin turning goals into measurable action.
  
  Focus areas include:
  
  * Resume development
  * Civilian job translation
  * Applications
  * Interview preparation
  * Financial literacy
  * Credit and budgeting
  * Education/training exploration
  * Benefits appointments
  * Communication skills
  * Life skills
  * Mentorship
  
  PHASE 3 — EXECUTE
  
  Days 46–84
  
  The emphasis moves toward implementation.
  
  Participants work toward:
  
  * Employment offers
  * School or training enrollment
  * Housing applications
  * Benefits connections
  * Savings and budgeting
  * Transportation planning
  * Community connections
  * Permanent support systems
  
  PHASE 4 — TRANSITION
  
  Days 85–90
  
  The final days focus on execution rather than classroom instruction.
  
  This can include:
  
  * Move-out
  * Final appointments
  * Housing transition
  * Document transfer
  * Transportation confirmation
  * Employer or school handoffs
  * Community-resource handoffs
  * Final transition review
  
  
  SECTION — A DAY AT STABLE GROUND
  
  Example program rhythm:
  
  0600
  Optional physical training
  
  0730
  Breakfast
  
  0900–1200
  Employment, education, projects, appointments or individual transition work
  
  1200
  Lunch
  
  1300–1700
  Classes, case management, mentorship, benefits navigation and life-skills programming
  
  1700
  Community dinner
  
  Evening
  Employment, study, independent living, appointments, recreation and individual responsibilities
  
  Programming will be adjusted where appropriate for employment, education, medical needs, disability, family responsibilities and individual transition plans.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — WHAT GRADUATION MEANS
  
  Heading
  
  90 DAYS ALONE DOESN’T EQUAL SUCCESS.
  
  Simply remaining in the program for 90 days is not the goal.
  
  Graduation is centered on documented progress toward sustainable independence.
  
  Participants should leave with:
  
  1. HOUSING
  
  Safe housing secured or a documented, realistic housing plan.
  
  2. INCOME PATH
  
  Employment, education, training, benefits-based income or another documented strategy.
  
  3. FINANCIAL READINESS
  
  A working monthly budget and forward financial plan.
  
  4. DAILY-LIFE SYSTEMS
  
  Identification, phone, transportation, records and appointment systems in place.
  
  5. SUPPORT NETWORK
  
  Identified people and organizations the veteran can contact after leaving.
  
  6. FUTURE DIRECTION
  
  A clear career or education plan with defined next actions.
  
  ⸻
  
  SECTION — AFTER GRADUATION
  
  Heading
  
  OUR RESPONSIBILITY DOESN’T END AT THE GATE.
  
  Planned follow-up points:
  
  30 Days
  
  90 Days
  
  180 Days
  
  365 Days
  
  OSG will use follow-up to evaluate housing stability, employment or education progress, resource connections and overall transition outcomes.

  SECTION — INITIAL ELIGIBILITY
  
  Applicants will generally need:
  
  * Verified U.S. military service
  * Honorable discharge
  * DD Form 214 or acceptable service documentation
  * Initial priority within approximately 180 days of separation
  * A documented transition need
  * Ability to participate safely in a nonclinical residential environment
  * Willingness to participate in the structured program
  * Completion of required screening
  
  
  
  SECTION — TRANSITION NEED
  
  Veterans may qualify based on issues including:
  
  • Homelessness or imminent homelessness
  • Temporary or unstable housing
  • Unemployment
  • Underemployment
  • Difficulty translating military experience
  • Financial instability
  • Limited support network
  • Social isolation
  • Difficulty accessing veteran resources
  • Need for civilian routine and structure
  • Other documented transition barriers
  
  ────────
  
  SECTION — HEALTH CONDITIONS
  
  Heading
  
  A DIAGNOSIS DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY DISQUALIFY A VETERAN.
  
  Veterans experiencing PTSD, depression, anxiety, physical disabilities, service-connected disabilities or other conditions will not be excluded solely because of a diagnosis.
  
  OSG is planned as a nonclinical residential transition program, however.
  
  Applicants requiring inpatient psychiatric treatment, medical detoxification, skilled nursing, secured treatment or another level of care OSG is not equipped or licensed to provide may be referred to an appropriate provider.
  
  ────────
  
  SECTION — CRIMINAL HISTORY
  
  A nonviolent criminal record does not automatically disqualify an applicant.
  
  OSG intends to use individualized safety and suitability review considering the circumstances, recency, rehabilitation, legal restrictions and ability of the program to safely support the applicant.

  SECTION — OUR STORY
  
  Operation Stable Ground grew from a simple question:
  
  What happens to veterans between leaving the military and finding their footing in civilian life?
  
  The military spends years teaching service members how to operate within a structured environment.
  
  Civilian life often begins with none of that structure.
  
  Operation Stable Ground was created to build a bridge between those two worlds.
  
  Not through handouts without direction.
  
  Not through housing alone.
  
  Through time, structure, accountability, community and opportunity.
