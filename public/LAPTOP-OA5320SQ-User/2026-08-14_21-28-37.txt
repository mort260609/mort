  # Detailed Study Notes from the Onboarding Guide
  
  ## 1. Main purpose of the onboarding guide
  
  The sheet is designed to train the **Client Success and Sales team** to handle Fiverr, Upwork, Shopify, WordPress, custom development, and other website-related inquiries.
  
  The main goal is to help team members:
  
  - Respond quickly to clients
  - Understand the client’s business and requirements
  - Identify the client’s budget, seriousness, and project complexity
  - Recommend the right service and pricing plan
  - Convert qualified leads into paying clients
  - Maintain proper communication during and after shifts
  - Coordinate with managers and teammates
  - Use internal tools and resources correctly
  
  The overall mindset is to be **client-focused, fast, responsible, helpful, and commercially aware**.
  
  ---
  
  ## 2. Important learning resources
  
  The sheet includes several learning materials that should be studied repeatedly.
  
  ### Main resources
  
  - Standard Operating Procedures, also called SOPs
  - Sales SOP
  - Client chat creation and communication training
  - Client-centric research for effective sales
  - Sales mindset training
  - Types of clients
  - How to interact and deal with clients
  - Ecommerce workflow
  - Winning product research
  - Shopify store creation process
  - Brand kit and proposal resources
  - Portfolio and previous project examples
  - Chat discussion examples
  - Brief handling and market research training
  
  ### Recommended study method
  
  Study the resources in this order
  
  1. Understand the company’s services
  2. Learn the sales and chat process
  3. Study examples of good and bad clients
  4. Review Shopify, WordPress, Wix, custom website, SaaS, and mobile app services
  5. Practice asking questions
  6. Practice preparing proposals and plans
  7. Study how to handle objections and negotiations
  8. Review previous project portfolios
  
  The guide repeatedly emphasizes that continuous learning is the employee’s responsibility. Questions should be asked immediately instead of waiting for a meeting.
  
  ---
  
  ## 3. Main objectives while talking to a client
  
  Every client conversation should help you understand four important things.
  
  ### Client background
  
  Find out who the client is, what business they operate, what industry they are in, and whether they already have a website, store, product, or business model.
  
  ### Client vision
  
  Understand what the client wants to build, redesign, improve, or sell.
  
  You should understand
  
  - Their business goal
  - Their target audience
  - How they expect to make money
  - What problem they are trying to solve
  - What result they expect from the project
  - Whether they need guidance or already know exactly what they want
  
  ### Project complexity
  
  Identify how difficult or time-consuming the project may be.
  
  Consider
  
  - Number of pages
  - Design requirements
  - Custom functionality
  - Payment or booking systems
  - Product count
  - Integrations
  - Membership features
  - Shipping and checkout requirements
  - Mobile responsiveness
  - SEO and conversion requirements
  - App or API integrations
  - Whether the project requires custom development
  
  ### Pricing and plan selection
  
  Pricing should be based on the client’s requirements, complexity, urgency, business type, platform, and expected value.
  
  Do not quote randomly. First understand the project, then recommend a suitable plan.
  
  ---
  
  ## 4. Client communication principles
  
  ### Respond quickly
  
  The guide treats response speed as extremely important.
  
  You should
  
  - Reply as quickly as possible
  - Avoid allowing the inquiry timer to continue unnecessarily
  - Start with a friendly and natural greeting
  - Keep the client engaged
  - Avoid long delays while researching
  - Continue the conversation with relevant questions
  
  A fast, useful reply is considered better than a slow reply that is grammatically perfect.
  
  ### Ask useful questions
  
  Questions should be simple, relevant, and easy for the client to understand.
  
  Good questions focus on
  
  - Business type
  - Main goal
  - Required platform
  - Number of pages or products
  - Desired features
  - Existing website or store
  - Target audience
  - Timeline
  - Budget range
  - Reference websites
  - Design preferences
  - Required integrations
  
  Avoid unnecessary technical questions that the client may not understand.
  
  ### Ask one question after another
  
  The recommended conversation style is
  
  1. Ask one useful question
  2. Study the client’s answer
  3. Ask the next relevant question
  4. Build a clear understanding of the project
  5. Move toward the right plan and price
  
  Do not ask a large list of unrelated questions at once.
  
  ### Be engaging
  
  The guide encourages a friendly and human tone.
  
  You should
  
  - Be polite
  - Use light humor when appropriate
  - Create an emotional connection
  - Show genuine interest
  - Understand the client’s business
  - Avoid robotic or obvious replies
  - Keep the conversation focused
  
  ---
  
  ## 5. Understanding different types of clients
  
  The sheet highlights four main client categories.
  
  ### Difficult clients
  
  Difficult clients may
  
  - Have unclear requirements
  - Change their mind repeatedly
  - Demand excessive revisions
  - Expect unrealistic timelines
  - Be difficult to communicate with
  - Ask for more work without increasing the budget
  
  These clients require careful communication, clear boundaries, and accurate documentation.
  
  ### Cheap or low-budget clients
  
  These clients may
  
  - Focus mainly on getting the lowest price
  - Compare many freelancers
  - Ask for large amounts of work at a very low budget
  - Negotiate continuously
  - Expect extra work for free
  
  The guide recommends identifying these clients early and protecting the company from unprofitable work.
  
  ### High-risk or problematic clients
  
  The sheet refers to these as black clients. In practical terms, these are clients who may create serious business, payment, communication, or project risks.
  
  Examples may include
  
  - Suspicious behavior
  - Unreasonable demands
  - Repeated disputes
  - Unclear payment expectations
  - Attempts to misuse the platform
  - Requests that violate company or platform rules
  
  Such clients should be handled carefully and escalated to a manager.
  
  ### High-budget clients
  
  High-budget clients may
  
  - Have an established business
  - Understand the value of professional work
  - Need advanced functionality
  - Have a clear business objective
  - Require long-term support
  - Be willing to invest in quality, strategy, and performance
  
  These clients should receive more detailed research, better recommendations, and stronger presentation of the company’s value.
  
  ---
  
  ## 6. Sales and pricing strategy
  
  ### Sell the complete solution
  
  The guide encourages presenting a complete plan instead of selling isolated small tasks.
  
  For example, if a client asks for a website fix or audit, the team may recommend a broader improvement plan that includes
  
  - Design improvements
  - Technical fixes
  - Conversion optimization
  - SEO improvements
  - Mobile optimization
  - Performance improvements
  - Post-launch support
  
  The proposal should focus on the client’s complete business outcome.
  
  ### Use multiple plans
  
  The training recommends presenting three plan options when suitable.
  
  A typical structure may include
  
  - Basic plan
  - Professional plan
  - Premium or advanced plan
  
  This helps the client compare value and makes the offer easier to understand.
  
  ### Be careful with discounts
  
  Negotiation should not happen too early.
  
  Before offering a discount, understand
  
  - The original plan price
  - The client’s budget
  - The project complexity
  - The amount of work required
  - Whether the client is likely to become profitable
  - Whether the client is trustworthy and cooperative
  
  Discounts should be limited and should not reduce the project below a reasonable value.
  
  ### Include extra work within the plan when practical
  
  Small complementary tasks may be included if they help close the order and do not significantly increase complexity.
  
  However, complex additional work should be added to the price and clearly included in the selected plan.
  
  ---
  
  ## 7. WordPress pricing and project classification
  
  The guide groups WordPress projects into four broad levels.
  
  ### Basic website
  
  Usually includes
  
  - Three to five pages
  - Simple business or portfolio website
  - No complex features
  
  Typical guide price
  
  - Around $600 to $800
  
  ### Professional business website
  
  Usually includes
  
  - Five to ten pages
  - Custom design sections
  - Forms
  - Basic integrations
  - More detailed branding
  
  Typical guide price
  
  - Around $800 to $1,500
  
  ### Advanced website
  
  Usually includes
  
  - More than ten pages
  - Booking systems
  - Membership features
  - Custom integrations
  - Advanced workflows
  
  Typical guide price
  
  - Around $1,500 to $3,000
  
  ### Ecommerce website
  
  Usually includes
  
  - Product catalog
  - Shopping cart
  - Payment gateways
  - Shipping setup
  - Product and checkout management
  
  Typical guide price
  
  - Around $1,500 to $4,000 or more
  
  Before quoting, understand the page count, design level, content responsibility, integrations, booking or payment features, and ecommerce requirements.

  ## 11. Use of AI tools
  
  The sheet includes guidance on using AI responsibly.
  
  ### Appropriate uses
  
  AI may help with
  
  - Understanding project complexity
  - Researching a platform
  - Explaining technical topics
  - Drafting proposals
  - Improving negotiation wording
  - Organizing information
  - Understanding client requirements
  - Preparing professional responses
  
  ### Important rule
  
  AI should support your work, not replace your own thinking or communication skills.
  
  You should
  
  - Understand the client personally
  - Read the entire conversation
  - Check the accuracy of the AI response
  - Adjust the wording to sound natural
  - Avoid blindly sending AI-generated text
  - Learn how to communicate without depending completely on AI
  
  The sheet specifically encourages employees to use AI for research and assistance, but not as a substitute for learning how to chat with clients.

  ## 12. Meeting procedure
  
  Client meetings are treated as important conversion opportunities.
  
  ### Before the meeting
  
  You should
  
  - Understand the client’s requirements
  - Review the previous chat
  - Prepare relevant portfolio examples
  - Confirm the meeting details
  - Join from a laptop
  - Use a professional account
  - Check microphone and audio
  - Inform the team in advance if you cannot attend
  
  ### During the meeting
  
  You should
  
  - Be professional and confident
  - Ask clear questions
  - Listen carefully
  - Explain the service in simple language
  - Build trust
  - Focus on the client’s business objective
  - Turn on the camera if appropriate
  - Avoid making unsupported promises
  
  ### Recording and documentation
  
  The guide requires the meeting to be recorded from beginning to end, including both microphone and system audio.
  
  After the meeting
  
  - Save the recording properly
  - Compress it if necessary
  - Use the required naming format
  - Share the meeting update with the group
  - Record important client details
  - Inform the relevant manager
  
  ---
  
  ## 13. Shift responsibilities
  
  Every team member is responsible for maintaining client continuity during their shift.
  
  ### During the shift
  
  You should
  
  - Stay focused on active chats
  - Respond quickly
  - Monitor assigned accounts
  - Prioritize important conversations
  - Avoid ignoring inquiries
  - Coordinate with teammates
  - Keep track of clients who may continue into the next shift
  
  ### At the end of the shift
  
  You should
  
  - Complete the tracking sheet
  - Record client usernames
  - Avoid duplicate entries
  - Hand over active conversations
  - Inform the next team member
  - Log out properly
  - Report important issues to the manager
  
  The guide makes it clear that every inquiry is the responsibility of the assigned employee during the shift.

  - Whether a meeting is scheduled
  
  The team should quickly identify which conversations require immediate attention.
  
  A client who has already shared detailed requirements, shown strong interest, or requested a meeting may deserve higher priority than a new low-information inquiry.

  # Digital Heroes Onboarding Guide - Detailed Summary
  
  ## DOCUMENT OVERVIEW
  This is a **comprehensive onboarding guide for Digital Heroes**, a web development and e-commerce agency. It serves as an operational manual for the client success and sales team handling projects on platforms like Fiverr and Upwork.
  
  ---
  
  ## KEY SECTIONS & EXPLANATIONS
  
  ### 1. **GIGS (SERVICE OFFERINGS)**
  The company offers **7 main services/gigs**:
  
  | Gig | Service Type | Description |
  |-----|--------------|-------------|
  | **Main Gig** | Shopify Store Design | Build, design, redesign ecommerce websites, dropshipping stores (TOP GIG) |
  | **Gig 3** | Full Stack Development | Full stack web developer for SaaS, apps, frontend, custom website development |
  | **Gig 4** | WordPress Development | Create WordPress website design, redesign, ecommerce website development |
  | **Gig 5** | Orange Shopify Gig | Shopify store design and website redesign for ecommerce development |
  | **Gig 6** | Purple Gig | Branded Shopify store design, website UI/UX development for ecommerce |
  | **Gig 7** | Black Gig | Branded Shopify store design, website UI/UX development for ecommerce |
  | **Gig 9** | Mobile Apps | Mobile app development with Flutter and React Native for iOS and Android |
  | **Gig 10** | Wix Development | Build Wix website development, create landing pages, Wix website design |
  
  ---
  
  ### 2. **CRITICAL KEYWORDS EXPLAINED**
  
  #### **A. OPERATIONAL TERMS**
  
  - **CRD (Chrome Remote Desktop)**: A virtual device access system. Team uses 6 devices (Device 1-6) for working remotely
  - **SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)**: Step-by-step instructions that should be reviewed repeatedly
  - **Brief**: Client's project requirements and specifications
  - **Trial Shift**: Initial 3-hour shift with 1-2 client handling to test capability
  - **Star Client**: High-quality, premium clients marked within minutes of inquiry
  - **GMC Gig**: A gig type they DO NOT take orders from (no acceptance)
  
  #### **B. CLIENT CLASSIFICATION**
  
  - **Star Client**: Quality, professional clients with good budget
  - **Non-Star Client**: Standard clients with regular requirements
  - **Difficult Client**: Tends to over-negotiate, requires special handling
  - **Black Client**: High-risk clients (scammers, hackers) - should be ignored
  - **Cheap Client**: Low budget, seeking maximum services at minimal cost
  - **High Budget Client**: Clients with significant investment capacity
  - **Sophisticated Client**: Professional, easy to work with
  - **Middle Man**: Intermediary/reseller clients
  
  #### **C. COMMUNICATION & TOOLS**
  
  - **Extension**: Browser plugin/tool (e.g., Koala Inspector, Shopify Spy)
  - **Gologin**: Account management tool for managing multiple profiles
  - **Claude**: AI tool used for understanding requirements (NOT for replying to clients directly)
  - **CRD (Clipboard functionality)**: Copy/paste shortcuts across devices (Win+V)
  - **Keyboard Shortcuts**:
    - Ctrl+C/V = Copy/Paste
    - Ctrl+A = Select All
    - Double Click = Line Selection
    - Tab+Alt or Tab+Win = Tab Selection
    - Screenshot available on clipboard
  
  #### **D. SALES & NEGOTIATION TERMS**
  
  - **Proposal**: Formal offer with pricing and deliverables
  - **Pitching**: Presenting pricing and services to client
  - **Negotiation**: Price discussion with client
  - **Discount**: Typically 5-10% reduction, max 15% without discussion
  - **CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization)**: Strategies to increase customer purchases
  - **PDP (Product Detail Page)**: Individual product pages on ecommerce site
  - **AOV (Average Order Value)**: Average amount customers spend per order
  
  #### **E. PROJECT MANAGEMENT**
  
  - **Quick Niche Store**: Rapid deployment store setup
  - **Portfolio Page**: Showcase of completed projects
  - **Responsiveness**: How quickly team responds to client inquiries
  - **Meeting Recording**: Mandatory for all custom client meetings
  - **Shift Rotation**: Everyone works different shifts monthly (Sunday+Saturday shifts available)
  - **Trail Shift**: Training period for new team members
  
  ---
  
  ### 3. **CRITICAL PROCEDURES**
  
  #### **CLIENT INQUIRY HANDLING**
  1. **60-Second Response Rule**: Reply within 60 seconds with "Hi, how are you?"
  2. **Research Phase**: Ask constructive, non-technical questions (NOT technical developer questions)
  3. **Information Gathering**:
     - Understand client's vision and how they'll make money
     - Check for duplicate entries in database
     - Gather business context and background
  4. **Proposal Creation**: After research is complete, create offers
  5. **Negotiation Strategy**:
     - Research client's budget from their proposal
     - Offer 5-10% discount from base plans
     - Maximum 15% discount without special approval
     - If budget is much lower, reorganize proposal but don't show drastic price drops
  
  #### **CLIENT TYPES & RESPONSES**
  
  **How to Recognize Different Clients:**
  - **Star Clients**: Come with clear vision, professional approach
  - **Cheap Clients**: Ask for maximum services at minimal cost
  - **Difficult Clients**: Over-negotiate, add scope creep
  - **Black Clients**: Scammers, hackers - DO NOT ENGAGE
  
  **Don't Negotiate With:**
  - Difficult clients
  - Black clients
  - Cheap clients
  - **Exception**: Pitch higher initially, then negotiate down for these types

  - **Team Meeting**: 3 PM to 5 PM (all team members)

  - **Handover Protocol**: Inform next shift person about ongoing clients
  - **Mandatory Recording**: Screen record ALL custom client meetings from start to finish
  - **FPS Setting**: Set Chrome Remote Desktop to 60 FPS for better responsiveness
  - **Backup Protocol**: If colleague misses shift, stay logged in and report to Manager (Divyansh)

  ## IMPORTANT RULES & GUIDELINES

  3. **Research thoroughly** before creating proposals
  4. **Screen record all custom meetings** (mandatory)

  8. **Check duplicate client entries** before responding
  9. **Use AI Claude** for understanding requirements (not for chat replies)
  10. **Prioritize by country, knowledge, project type & conversation stage**
  
  ### ❌ **DON'Ts**
  
  1. **Don't reply instantly** using AI/Claude - chat yourself to improve
  2. **Don't create offers immediately** - research first
  3. **Don't negotiate** with difficult/black/cheap clients excessively
  4. **Don't use Claude** for direct client communication
  5. **Don't ask technical developer questions** to clients
  6. **Don't share device access** - coordinate through the group
  7. **Don't ignore ongoing clients** if colleague misses shift
  8. **Don't play audio/video** on CRD devices unless necessary
  9. **Don't miss team meetings** (3-5 PM)
  10. **Don't go off-track** from sales scripts
