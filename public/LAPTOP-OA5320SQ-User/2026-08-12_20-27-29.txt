  THE MIXED CART QUESTION, DIRECTLY
  
  The two offers evaluate independently. Front Only cards count toward the Front bundle, Story cards count toward the Story bundle, and they never pool. That is the correct behavior commercially, because pooling would let someone put three Story cards in and pay fifteen euros instead of twenty-five.
  
  So with two Front and two Story in the cart, neither bundle completes and no discount applies. Technically fine. The problem is the customer has four cards, no discount, and no idea why.
  
  The answer is showing both bundles separately in the cart, each with its own progress. Front Only: two of three, add one more to save four euros fifty. Story: two of three, add one more to save two euros.
  
  And here is the part I like. Look at the actual maths of your pricing. Two Front cards is thirteen euros, three is fifteen, so the third Front card costs two euros. Two Story cards is eighteen, three is twenty-five, so the third Story card costs seven.
  
  That means the cart can always tell someone the cheapest way to finish a bundle, and it is nearly always the Front Only one. Add one more Front Only card for just two euros is a genuinely compelling line, and it is true rather than a sales trick. Your pricing structure creates that opportunity by itself.
