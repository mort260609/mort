  LOGIXBRIDGE
  90-Day Business Development SOP
  Channel 4 System (Audit-Based Outreach) — Step-by-Step, Hour-by-Hour, 6 Hours/Day
  This version replaces the earlier draft with a single, deep, step-by-step operating system built entirely around Channel 4 — 
  Website/Google-Profile Audit Outreach — which absorbs Channels 1–3 (LinkedIn, Google Maps, Cold Email) as sourcing 
  and delivery methods for the same audit-first approach. Channel 5 (Upwork/Fiverr/Contra) is intentionally held back and 
  introduced in Month 3, once the core system is proven. Every activity fits inside a strict 6-hour day.
  1. The Core System — How Every Piece Connects
  This is one continuous funnel, not five separate channels. Google Maps and LinkedIn are just two different doors into 
  the same process:
  1.  SOURCE — find a real business (Google Maps) and its decision-maker (LinkedIn) in your target niche/city.
  2.  AUDIT — check their website and Google profile for 2–3 concrete, damaging issues.
  3.  COMPARE — find their top 3 local competitors and the gap between them (reviews, rankings, traffic estimate).
  4.  REACH OUT — send one message referencing the audit + competitor gap (via LinkedIn DM and/or email).
  5.  FOLLOW UP — a 6-touch sequence over 25 days if no reply.
  6.  BOOK — schedule the discovery call for the owner once they respond positively.
  7.  LOG — every action recorded in the tracker the same day.
  Doing this well for 6–8 well-qualified prospects a day, every working day, is what makes the math in Section 11 work. 
  Volume without the audit step gets ignored; the audit step is what makes messages get replies.
  2. The 6-Hour Day — Exact Hour-by-Hour Breakdown
  Recommended shift: 6:00 PM – 12:00 AM PKT (covers US East Coast morning through Central/Mountain midday — 
  see timezone logic in Section 12). If the owner prefers a split schedule, Block A can move earlier in the day since it 
  doesn't require US business hours.

  3. Step-by-Step: Sourcing on Google Maps
  1.  Open Google Maps in an incognito/private window (so results aren't personalized by past searches).
  2.  Search: "[service] + [city]" — e.g. "garage door repair Phoenix."
  3.  Ignore the top 3 (map-pack) results — they're already winning. Scroll to positions 4–15 in the list below the map.
  4.  Open each candidate's listing. Note: review count, star rating, number of photos, whether "Website" button exists.
  5.  Click through to the website. In under 30 seconds, judge: does it load fast, is it mobile-friendly, does the homepage 
  mention the service + city clearly?
  6.  Keep only businesses with under ~50 reviews AND a visibly weak or missing website — these are the highest
  probability targets.
  7.  Repeat for 2–3 cities per day from your assigned city list (Section 12) until you have 10–12 candidates.
  8.  Paste business name + city + Maps link + website URL into the tracker's "New Lead" rows.
  4. Step-by-Step: Sourcing the Decision-Maker on LinkedIn
  1.  Take each shortlisted business name from Google Maps and search it directly on LinkedIn.
  2.  If the business page doesn't show clearly, search: "[Owner OR Founder OR CEO] + [service] + [city]."
  3.  Use LinkedIn's People filters: Location → target country; Industry → matching category; Company size → 1–50 
  employees.
  4.  Open the top matching profile. Confirm the title is Owner / Founder / CEO / President / Marketing Director — not a 
  generic employee.
  5.  Check if they've posted or commented in the last 30 days ("Recently Active") — prioritize these; they reply faster.
  6.  Copy the profile URL into the tracker next to that business's row.
  7.  If no matching decision-maker is found on LinkedIn within 3–4 minutes, don't force it — fall back to email-only 
  outreach for that business (Section 6) and move on.
  Time cap: no more than 4 minutes per business on LinkedIn search. Spending longer kills your daily volume — if it's not 
  findable quickly, move on.
  5. Step-by-Step: Competitor Research & Gap Estimate
  1.  Re-run the same incognito Google search from Section 3 Step 2. This time, note the top 3 map-pack businesses — 
  these are the target's direct competitors.
  2.  Open each competitor's Google listing: review count, rating, number of photos, completeness of "Services" section.
  3.  Open each competitor's website: check for dedicated pages per suburb/service that your target doesn't have.
  4.  Check for paid ads: search the same term and look for the small "Sponsored" label above the map — note if the 
  competitor (not your target) is running ads.
  5.  Go to Ubersuggest (free tier) → enter the competitor's domain → note estimated monthly organic traffic and top 
  ranking keywords.
  6.  Enter your target's own domain in the same tool → compare the traffic numbers side by side.
  7.  Translate the traffic gap into a plain-English estimate for the pitch, e.g.: "[Competitor] gets roughly 300 more 
  monthly visits than you — even at a modest 5% call rate, that's close to 15 extra jobs a month going to them instead 
  of you."
  Keep the language to "roughly," "an estimated," "could be worth" — never state exact revenue figures you can't verify. 
  Directional and honest is more credible than a suspiciously precise fake number.
  6. Step-by-Step: The Mistake Audit Checklist
  Run this checklist on every shortlisted target's website + Google Business Profile. Takes 3–5 minutes once you're used 
  to it.
  ☐ Homepage title/headline doesn't include "[service] + [city]"
  ☐ No dedicated landing pages for nearby suburbs/service areas
  ☐ Google PageSpeed Insights score under 50 (test both mobile + desktop)
  ☐ Site isn't clearly mobile-optimized (text/buttons too small, layout breaks)
  ☐ No obvious call-to-action — phone number not visible above the fold, no "Book Now"/"Get Quote" button
  ☐ Google Business Profile has fewer than 10 photos or an incomplete Services list
  ☐ Few recent reviews (nothing in the last 60–90 days) or no owner replies to existing reviews
  ☐ No visible lead-capture form or click-to-call button on mobile
  Pick the top 2–3 most damaging issues only — don't list all 8 in the outreach message. Overloading the prospect with 
  problems reads as generic and gets ignored; 2–3 specific, real ones read as "you actually looked at my business."
  7. Step-by-Step: Writing & Sending the Outreach Message
  Formula: [1 specific observation] + [1 competitor comparison] + [soft, low-commitment offer]. Never open with "we 
  do SEO/websites/marketing."
  LinkedIn DM template
  Hi [Name] — I noticed [Business Name] isn't showing up for a few nearby-area searches where [Competitor] is capturing 
  the traffic, and your Google profile is missing [specific gap]. Worth a quick look at what's driving that?
  Email template (audit-based)
  Subject: Quick find on [Business Name]'s Google listing
  Hi [Name],
  I took a look at [Business Name]'s Google Business Profile and website while researching [service] companies in [City]. A 
  couple of things stood out:– [Mistake 1]– [Mistake 2]– [Competitor] has [X] more reviews and shows up in the map 3-pack; you currently don't
  I can send a short breakdown of what I'd fix first — no obligation. Want me to send it over?
  1.  Confirm which channel each target is on (LinkedIn profile found in Section 4? Use LinkedIn + email if you have 
  both).
  2.  Fill the template with the specific mistakes and competitor gap from Sections 5–6 — never send a template with 
  placeholders left in.
  3.  Re-read once for tone — it should sound like a real observation from a person, not copy-paste.
  4.  Send. Log the exact date/time sent in the tracker as "Day 1" of the follow-up sequence.

  "Should I close this out, or is timing just off?" —

  10. Common Mistakes to Avoid (BD Side, Not Just the Prospect's Website)
  • Sending a generic "we do SEO/websites/ads" opener — always lead with a specific, real observation.
  • Listing every audit issue instead of the top 2–3 — overwhelms and reads as templated.
  • Giving up after Day 1 — most replies come from Day 6–25 touches, not the first message.
  • Repeating "just following up" — every follow-up must add a new detail or angle.
  • Spending too long per prospect (over 10–12 min total) — kills daily volume; cap each step per Sections 3–6.
  • Not logging same-day — a lead not in the tracker the day it's touched gets lost or double-messaged.
  • Quoting exact competitor revenue numbers you can't verify — use "estimated/roughly" language instead.
  • Skipping the LinkedIn time cap (Section 4) and going down a research rabbit hole on one contact.

  • Drop or reduce anything under a 3–4% response rate.
  • Use your first 1–2 closed clients as a mini case study line in the Day 10 follow-up message.
  • Tighten the call-prep brief (Block D) so the owner has full context before every call.
